# Spend the expiring credit testing discovery

The engine's next bottleneck is no longer the editor or the judges; it is **finding the right hypothesis without being handed it**, and the Claude API capabilities worth adopting are the ones that make research and blind discovery cheap and checkable. For Step 3's design, adopt five things: **Message Batches** for paper triage and extraction (50% off, stacks with caching), **1-hour prompt caching** of the rubric and playbook prefix, **citations in a separate pass from structured outputs** (they cannot share a request), **an explicit per-role `effort` setting**, and **allowlisted web search/fetch** as a complement to, not a replacement for, the hand-built arXiv/Semantic Scholar client. Skip the memory tool, MCP connector, tool search, Managed Agents, Agent Skills, computer use and fast mode for now; none improves what the frozen judges can prove. With about **$197 of the $200 left and roughly 30 hours before it expires**, the highest-value spend is not on new features but on answering the questions Step 3's design hinges on: does higher effort improve *blind* hypothesis generation, does retrieved technique context raise the *executed* win rate over the model alone, and what do papers really cost per page. A nine-step plan (seven paid experiments plus free checks and write-up) costs about **$45–90**, leaves a buffer, and turns part of the credit into durable assets (a calibrated effort policy, a seed set of cited playbook cards, a labelled triage set). Two caveats shape everything: the existing Opus-vs-Sonnet result was **not like for like** (Opus 5.5 ran at its `medium` default, Sonnet 5.5 at its `high` default, because the engine sets no effort), and the literature has **no controlled evidence that paper retrieval raises executed optimization wins**, so Step 3 should be built to find that out rather than assume it.

## The 12/12 result measured the editor, so discovery is the open question

Handed the right hypothesis, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 each won 12/12 slowdowns with 0/3 false wins, at $0.10 and $0.045 per run. That saturates the handed-hypothesis corpus; the repo's own `STATUS.md` notes it "measures the editor and the judges, not discovery". The outside evidence says discovery is where agents fail: on SWE-fficiency, agents reached under 0.23x of the expert speedup at release, and OpenHands found the dominant failure was localisation, with the wrong file or function targeted **about 71% of the time** ([arXiv 2511.06090](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.06090); [OpenHands](https://openhands.dev/blog/20260216-swefficiency-benchmark)). The engine's cProfile step plus a precondition matcher attacks exactly that failure.

The comparison also needs re-running under control. Effort defaults to **`medium` on Opus 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 but `high` on Sonnet 5.5**, and omitting it is identical to sending the default ([Effort](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort); [Models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview)). The understand request and the editor send no `output_config` (`services/python_agents/src/autonomous_ai_agents/orchestrator/understand.py`, `execution/editor.py`), so the ledger compared Opus-medium with Sonnet-high. Anthropic's own sweeps suggest effort matters for coding and is mixed for research: Opus 5.5 on a SWE-bench Pro subset went from **87.4% at low ($0.11) to 92.8% at medium ($0.21), 95.3% at high ($0.29) and 96.7% at xhigh ($0.74)**, while Fable 5.1 gained nothing from low to high on DeepResearch Bench II and Opus 5.5 lost 13.3 points at low on DRACO ([Optimizing for cost and intelligence](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)). The same page reports that running at low and **re-running failures at high solved about 97% at about $0.17 per task**, beating everything-at-high, but only with "a failure signal" that does not pass bad work ([Optimizing for cost and intelligence](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)). The engine's frozen judges, N >= 5 noise rule and capture rules are that failure signal, which makes "cheap first, escalate on reject or inconclusive" a natural fit for the existing `MAX_TRIAL_ROUNDS=3` loop.

Retrieval is the lever for going beyond the model's knowledge, because every current model has a **reliable knowledge cutoff of June 2026** ([Models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview)), so anything the arXiv monitor finds from July onward is unknown to the model. But the strongest study of executed ideas found that LLM ideas' scores fell significantly more than human ideas' once executed, flipping the ranking on many metrics ([arXiv 2506.20803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20803)). Every system that produced verified novel results, from FunSearch to AlphaEvolve and ShinkaEvolve, pairs a generator with an executable evaluator and a scored archive, and the production retrieval-driven system, Google's ECO, works from a curated anti-pattern catalogue with verification and human review ([AlphaEvolve](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/); [ShinkaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19349v1); [ECO, arXiv 2503.15669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15669)). That argues for a playbook first, papers as a feed into the same card format, and paper-derived hypotheses ranked by verdicts and lessons, never by an LLM's novelty rating.

Two structural points sit behind the table. First, **untrusted text should arrive in `tool_result` blocks**, with a stated untrusted-content policy and an optional Haiku 5.5 structured-boolean injection screen that treats a refusal as a positive verdict ([Mitigate jailbreaks](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/mitigate-jailbreaks)). `search_result` blocks inside a `tool_result` remain citable ([Search results](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/search-results)), which reconciles that guidance with the citations doc's examples that put documents in plain user turns. Second, citations turn grounding into a judge-like check: for each `page_location`, extract those pages locally and assert that the normalized `cited_text` is a substring, rejecting any claim without a citation or whose numbers are absent from its quote. That mirrors the engine's frozen-judge philosophy at the research layer.

## Eight constraints already narrow the Step 3 design

The researchers found limits that would otherwise surface as 400 errors or silent cost drift. **Citations and structured outputs cannot share a request**: "If you enable citations on any user-provided document ... and also include the `output_config.format` parameter ... the API returns a 400 error" ([Citations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)), so extraction must run as a cited-prose pass followed by a document-free structuring pass, in separate cache prefixes. Since web search citations are always on, web search plus `output_config.format` in one request will probably fail too; that is untested. **Batches reject `fallbacks`** ([Batch processing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/batch-processing)), and **Haiku 5.5 has no server-side fallback at all**: a model list returns a 400 ([Refusals and fallback](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/refusals-and-fallback)), so batched jobs need client-side retry of failed `custom_id`s. Forced `tool_choice` (`any`/`tool`) is unsupported on Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 ([Parallel tool use](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/parallel-tool-use)), so use `auto` with `strict: true` tools or structured outputs. Any schema field named or described as `reasoning`, `thinking` or `trace` can trigger a **billed `reasoning_extraction` refusal** ([Refusals and fallback](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/refusals-and-fallback)); call it `justification_summary`.

The environment imposes its own limits. **This cloud container cannot reach arXiv, Semantic Scholar or OpenAlex**: the egress proxy refused CONNECT to all three on 2026-10-23, so live tests of the hand-built client must run from CI or the user's machine. Server-side web search and fetch run on Anthropic's infrastructure, so they should work from here even though direct egress is blocked; that is an untested inference, and arXiv's `robots.txt` treatment of Anthropic's fetcher is unknown. The literature APIs themselves are slow by design: arXiv allows one request every 3 seconds ([arXiv API ToU](https://info.arxiv.org/help/api/tou.html)), Semantic Scholar one per second with a key ([S2 tutorial](https://webflow.semanticscholar.org/product/api/tutorial)), and OpenAlex is now usage-priced at $1 a day free with a key ([OpenAlex](https://developers.openalex.org/api-reference/authentication)).

The price table has two errors. `MODEL_PRICING["claude-haiku-5-5"]` carries `cache_read=0.10` (`services/python_agents/src/autonomous_ai_agents/common/config.py`, line 53), while the docs say **$0.01** ([Pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)); that overstates cached Haiku spend tenfold, which is safe for the budget but distorts cost comparisons. The table also lacks Haiku's **over-100K tier ($0.50 / $2.50)**, which would *under*-price any long triage prompt and so weaken the hard cost cap. Two smaller notes: the bundled `claude-api` skill reference (cached 2026-10-06) disagrees with the live docs on the Sonnet 5.5 cache-read price ($0.20 vs $0.10) and on whether Opus 5.5 fast mode needs a beta header, and the live docs win in both cases ([Pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [Fast mode](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/fast-mode)). For Step 5, optimizing the user's VLM code could trip the `frontier_llm` refusal category, which "benign machine learning work can also trigger" and which is billed even with no output ([Refusals and fallback](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/refusals-and-fallback)); log `stop_details.category` per hypothesis. The `claude-opus-4-8` fallback stays valid until at least 2027-05-28, but Opus 5.5 thinking blocks are dropped on it ([Model deprecations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations); [What's new in Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/opus-5-5/whats-new-opus-5-5)).

## A 30-hour plan that buys answers, not features

Order matters for two reasons: batches can take up to 24 hours, so submit them early, and the blind experiments need a hand-written playbook that must be drafted *before* anyone looks at the corpus's planted fixes. Every experiment runs under the existing live conventions (`live` marker, `LIVE_SUITE_CAP_USD`, `-n 0`), sends no user repository content to the server tools, and deletes Files API outputs afterwards. Costs are the researchers' estimates from documented prices; measure one request with the free `count_tokens` before scaling each run.

The plan totals roughly **$45–90**, leaving over $100 unspent. That is deliberate: the remaining credit is only worth spending where it produces evidence or durable assets, and the best candidates for extra spend are more non-obvious repos in step 5 (statistical power comes from more paired tasks, not more repetitions of the same 15) and the 50-paper extraction batch in step 7, whose cited claims become candidate playbook cards that outlive the credit. Spending to the limit on repetition alone would buy little.

For **Step 3's design**, adopt: per-role model and effort settings (understand Opus medium; hypotheses per the sweep; editor Sonnet with Opus-high escalation; triage Haiku low), with effort fixed per conversation to protect the cache; Batches with 1 h caching and client-side refusal retry for triage and extraction; two-pass citation-then-structure extraction with local `cited_text` verification; papers delivered as `tool_result` content with an untrusted-content policy and a Haiku screen; allowlisted web search/fetch in a research request with no editor or bash tools, mirrored by an org-level Console allowlist; `strict: true` on new tools; an evaluation cascade (preconditions, diff guards, tests and output equivalence, then N >= 5 timing); a lessons-backed archive with "inspirations" in the hypothesis prompt and near-duplicate rejection before trials, the ShinkaEvolve idea ([arXiv 2509.19349](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19349v1)); and a ledger that records `thinking_tokens`, `server_tool_use` and refusal categories. Any package name a paper or search result surfaces goes through the existing approval-gated vetting.

**Skip** for now: the memory tool, which is only a client-side file schema and would let the model write un-evaluated "lessons", a reward-hacking channel ([Memory tool](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/memory-tool)); the MCP connector, which is beta, not ZDR, and offers only community arXiv and Semantic Scholar servers ([MCP connector](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/mcp-connector)); tool search until there are 10+ tools ([Tool search tool](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/tool-search-tool)); Managed Agents for the trial path, since cloud sandboxes are x86_64 Linux and self-hosted workers need a Linux host, so neither can measure an M4 Pro ([Cloud sandbox reference](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/cloud-sandboxes-reference); [Self-hosted sandboxes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/self-hosted-sandboxes)); the Agent SDK in production, because it loads `.claude/` configuration that a user's repository can supply ([Agent SDK overview](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/overview)); Agent Skills, computer use, and context editing or compaction for single-shot research calls; and fast mode, which doubles price for faster output while trial wall time is dominated by measurement ([Fast mode](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/fast-mode)).

## Conclusion

The engine already owns the part that published systems found hardest: a fitness function that resists reward hacking. Sakana's AI CUDA Engineer saw claimed speedups collapse once its harness was fixed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/21/sakana-walks-back-claims-that-its-ai-can-dramatically-speed-up-model-training/embed/)), and AI Scientist v2's accepted paper drew findings of faked results ([arXiv 2504.08066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.08066)). That changes how to read the Claude API catalogue. The frozen judges make cheap, fallible generation safe, so the right capabilities are the ones that widen the search cheaply (batches, caching, low effort with escalation, allowlisted search) and the ones that make research claims checkable before they cost a trial (citations verified against local page text). Features that move execution or memory off the user's machine work against the engine's core value, however convenient.

The expiring credit is best treated as a research grant for one question the literature leaves open: whether retrieved techniques produce more *executed, judged* wins than the model's own ideas. If the A/B says yes, Step 3 builds the paper pipeline with confidence; if it says no, the cheaper playbook, lessons and evolutionary loop carry the "beyond the model" goal, and the paper feed becomes a slow source of candidate cards that a human approves. Either answer is worth more than the $20–40 it costs.
